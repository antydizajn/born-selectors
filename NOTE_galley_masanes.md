# Relation to Galley–Masanes (1610.04859)

**Date:** 2026-07-24  
**Paper:** Galley & Masanes, "Classification of all alternatives to the Born rule in terms of informational properties", arXiv:1610.04859

## What they do

Keep pure states = rays in CP^{d-1} and unitary dynamics. Classify all alternative measurement postulates such that mixed states have finitely many parameters. Alternatives ↔ representations of the unitary group. Many non-Born theories exist. Unrestricted non-quantum ones violate bit symmetry. Born is singled out among unrestricted theories by bit symmetry (and related properties).

## Relation to our paper

| | Galley–Masanes | Our paper |
|--|----------------|-----------|
| Pure-state structure | assumed (rays + unitaries) | assumed (via Herm_d setup) |
| Measurement class | all finite-param OPFs | **only bilinear** forms on Herm×Herm |
| Main output | full classification of alternatives | thin slice $B_{a,b}$, then selectors → Born |
| Strength of Born uniqueness | bit symmetry etc. among unrestricted alternatives | elementary selectors inside bilinear class |

**Honest positioning:** We do **not** improve on Galley–Masanes' general classification. We study a **special case** where probabilities extend bilinearly to mixed states and effects. In that case the classification collapses to a 1-parameter family after normalization, and several trivial-looking operational conditions finish the job.

## Why the special case is still publishable as a short note

1. Bilinear extension is the standard mixed-state Born pairing's natural ambient class.
2. Selector catalogue (impossible event ≡ pure certainty; monoidality; dim-independence; ancilla fails) is clean pedagogy.
3. Explicit layer relative to Nakahira (OPT reconstruction) and Hossenfelder (pure continuous).
